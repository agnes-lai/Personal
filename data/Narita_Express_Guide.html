Ginza → Narita hotel, by Narita Express

The whole run from Ginza back to the hotel by Narita: buying the ticket, finding the train, and the hotel shuttle at the other end. Every step folds away — skim the titles when you're in a hurry.

Before you leave Ginza

Ginza has no JR station. The Narita Express leaves from Tokyo Station — walk or take one metro stop from Ginza first, then board N'EX there.

  • Ginza → Tokyo Station: about 10 min on foot, or one stop (~3 min) on the Marunouchi line
  • N'EX Tokyo → Narita Airport: about 60 minutes, no changes
  • It stops at T2·3 first, then T1 — which one you get off at depends on your hotel's bus stop, see the shuttle section
  • N'EX is all reserved seating, no unreserved cars — you must hold a limited-express ticket
Cheaper alternatives: Route A in the main guide (Toei Asakusa through-train) is about ¥1,150–1,400 and Route B (JR Sōbu rapid) about ¥1,340. N'EX costs roughly double — what you buy is no transfers, luggage racks and a guaranteed seat.

Step by step

1Follow the JR signs — look for the red N’EX badgeGreen JR square, then the red N’EX box next to it▾

The overhead signs pair the green JR square with a small red NEX badge. That red badge is the thing to lock onto — it means Narita Express specifically, as opposed to ordinary JR lines.

2Confirm the red N’EX platform sign成田エクスプレス · Car No. 1–7▾

The platform sign reads 成田エクスプレス / Narita Express with the same red NEX badge on the left and the car range on the right (for example Car No. 1–7).

  • Your ticket shows a car number and seat — match the car number first, the platform floor is marked
  • The same platform also serves the Sōbu rapid (white with a blue stripe). N’EX is white with a red stripe — check the body before you board
3Keep walking past the Keisei signsBlue KS logo = Keisei, a different company▾

You'll pass signs for the Keisei Line (blue KS logo) — Skyliner and Access Express. That's a different company and an N’EX ticket is not valid on it. If you bought N’EX, keep walking toward JR.

Why this is here: JR and Keisei sit on the same level a short distance apart, so it’s easy to walk into the wrong gates. Both go to Narita Airport, but the tickets are not interchangeable.
4Ticket machines are in zone B, on the rightLook for the big white letter B▾

Look for the large white letter B on the wall. That bay is the Narita Express self-service ticket machines, with 成田エクスプレス Narita Express written above them.

  • The machines have an English mode — press English at the top right first
  • A “NEX TOKYO Round Trip Ticket” board often stands beside them — that’s the return product, not for a one-way
5Buy at the red machineRed = N’EX. Pick date, train, seats▾

Use the red machines — they carry the red NEX badge above them. Choose date, train and number of passengers, and the machine assigns your reserved seats.

  • Press 领収書 / Receipt before finishing — it’s awkward to get one afterwards, and you’ll want it for the expense split
  • The machines take cash and credit cards
  • You may get two tickets (basic fare + limited express) or one combined — both are needed at the entry and exit gates, so don’t bin them at the airport
6Board, then get off at the right terminalT2·3 comes first, T1 is the last stop▾

About 60 minutes to the airport. T2·3 comes first, T1 is the terminus. Which one you want depends entirely on where your hotel’s shuttle stops — see the shuttle section below.

  • At the exit gate you still need the ticket(s) — keep both until you’re through
  • From the JR gates go up to the departures level; the shuttle stops are outside the terminal at numbered bus bays

Ticket rules — why there are two tickets

N'EX needs two things: a basic fare ticket (乘车券) and a limited-express ticket (特急券). That's standard for Japanese limited expresses — one pays for the distance, the other for riding the fast train and holding a reserved seat.

  • Normal case: the machine sells both at once, then you just walk in.
  • No basic fare ticket? You can tap in with Suica and hold a separate limited-express ticket — the Suica covers the basic fare part.
  • No limited-express ticket? Tap in with Suica, board, and pay the conductor on the train.
  • But buying on board risks no seat, or a surcharge, and evening airport-bound trains fill up — buy before boarding if you can.
In short: you can't get stranded — as long as you have a Suica with enough balance, there is always a way onto the train.

Treat the machine as the source of truth: JR revised these fares in March 2026, and the sources I found sit between ¥3,070 and ¥3,140 — I can't tell which applies on your date. Budget about ¥3,100 per person and trust the screen.
  • Season shifts the limited-express part: −¥200 off-peak, +¥200 peak, +¥400 highest peak. Early October is normally standard season.
  • Four people ≈ ¥12,400, roughly ¥6,000 more than Route A or B. That's the price of not changing trains with luggage.
Getting a receipt:
  • At the machine: press 领収書 / Receipt on screen before the transaction finishes and it prints one separately.
  • The Suica leg has no paper receipt — but any JR machine can print your usage history (利用履歴, up to 100 entries).
  • Top-ups can be receipted too — the same 领収書 option appears when charging a card.
  • Photograph it on the spot and log it straight into the guide's split or tabung — paper receipts get lost in airports.

Suica — how much to load, where to buy

If you plan to tap in with Suica and buy only the limited-express ticket, the card must hold at least the basic fare portion.

A short balance traps you at the exit gate: Entry only checks that you have money; the full fare comes off at the exit. If you're short you must use the Fare Adjustment machine beside the gate — awkward with luggage.
Where to buy a Suica other than the airport
  • Good news: ordinary green Suica sales resumed on 1 March 2025 and stock has been stable through 2026.
  • JR multifunction ticket machines (black or green screens, English mode) — Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ueno are all reliable.
  • JR EAST Travel Service Centers: Tokyo, Shinagawa, Shibuya, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, Ueno.
  • Deposit ¥500, refundable at a counter (minus a ¥220 handling fee from the balance; no fee if the balance is under ¥220).
  • Welcome Suica (the red one) is non-refundable and valid 28 days — if that's what you get, don't load a large sum.
  • Android caveat: phones sold outside Japan generally cannot install Mobile Suica (an NFC limitation). iPhones can add one to Apple Wallet directly.
  • Topping up: station machines (cash only for physical cards), staffed windows, or any konbini counter — 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson. Hand over the card and say “chāji onegaishimasu”.
