One place you can’t: Fox Village

No selfie stick at Zao Fox Village (Day 3). The foxes roam free, an extended pole spooks them and can get bitten. Shoot handheld that day and leave the stick on the bus.
Everywhere else on the trip is fine. Goshikinuma, the viewpoint at the far end of Ouchi-juku and Hitachi Seaside Park all suit it — tripod plus remote means nobody sits out the group photo. Indoors and on platforms, just follow the signs at the entrance.

If it won’t work

TO FILLAnything that went wrong while you were testing it, and what fixed it. The ones worth writing down are usually: remote stops responding, phone won’t pair again after being switched off, shutter opens the wrong app, legs won’t hold the phone up.
One that applies to all of them: The remote pairs with one phone at a time. If it silently reconnects to a travel companion’s phone, your shutter press does nothing — have them disconnect first.

Flying with it

  • The stick itself normally travels in cabin baggage, though airlines differ — folded it’s short, so it rarely reads as a “baton”.
  • The remote’s coin cell or built-in lithium battery belongs in the cabin, not in checked baggage — same rule as a power bank.
  • Pack it with your electronics so it’s quick to pull out at Narita security on the way home.
  • Charge and pair it before you fly — discovering it needs a battery you don’t have is a bad moment in rural Tohoku.
