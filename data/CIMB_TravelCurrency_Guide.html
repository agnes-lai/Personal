CIMB TravelCurrency — ordering yen before you fly

Order online, collect the cash at the airport. Every step folds away. The thing that actually matters is the cut-off below.

The cut-off

Order before 4:00pm, Sat 3 Oct 2026
The order page states: orders placed before 4:00pm can be collected the next day. You fly early on 4 Oct, so 3 Oct at 4pm is the last moment.
  • Order a day or two earlier if you can — that leaves room to redo it if the IC is rejected, payment fails, or something needs changing.
  • Ordering on the 4th itself will not work — same-day orders are only collectable the following day.
  • The details arrive by email after ordering and you need them to collect — check your spam folder if it doesn’t show up.

Step by step

1Open CIMB Clicks → TravelCurrencyLog in to CIMB Clicks first▾

Log in to CIMB Clicks, then open the TravelCurrency page.

cimbclicks.com.my → TravelCurrency ↗

  • The person ordering must be the account holder — the name is checked again at collection.
2Pick JPY and type the amountType either side — yen or ringgit▾

Choose JPY – JAPANESE YEN under “I want to order”, then type the yen amount — the ringgit side fills in automatically, and it works in reverse too.

  • Minimum RM100 per currency.
  • Amounts are rounded to the nearest note — yen usually comes in ¥1,000 units, so a round 120,000 fits exactly.
  • Note the rate line (e.g. 100 JPY = 2.6290 MYR) — the rate-check section below uses it.
3Buy Now, then ContinueCheck the total before continuing▾

Press Buy Now to reach the order summary. Check Total Order, then press Continue.

  • One order can hold several currencies — you transit at Changi, so if anyone wants SGD, add it here rather than placing a second order and collecting twice.
4Collection details — the part that gets rejectedName and IC must match the account▾

Fill in name, IC, mobile and email, then choose the collection date and branch.

  • The collector must turn up in person — nobody can collect on your behalf.
  • The name and IC must match the account holder — the bank can refuse to hand over the cash if they don’t.
  • Mobile number needs the country code, e.g. 60123456789.
  • A wrong IC format is flagged immediately (the red cross in the screenshot) — type all 12 digits, no dashes.
  • Get the email right — the order details are emailed to you and you need them at the counter.
5Declaration, then payTick T&C, answer the marketing question▾

Tick Terms & Conditions, then pick whether you consent to marketing (either answer is fine and does not affect the order). Press Continue to pay.

  • Check the Order Summary once more after paying: currency, amount, RM total.
  • Screenshot the confirmation and log the amount in the guide’s tabung or split — if this is the group’s shared cash, this is where the pool starts.
6Collect at Penang airportLevel 2, landside — before security▾

Pick the cash up at Penang airport on the date you chose. Bring your IC or passport and the order email.

  • Landside means before security. You reach the airport at 7:00 on Day 1 and the counter opens at 6:00 — collect first, then check in.
  • Collect before you go through — once you’re airside you cannot come back for it.
  • Count it at the counter, especially how many ¥10,000 notes you get — plenty of small shops in Japan won’t break one.

Is the rate any good?

The order page shows 100 JPY = something MYR. To judge it, compare that number against the mid-market rate on the day.

Source100 JPY → MYR

That spread is very tight. Bank counters usually take 1.5–3%. But these two numbers are from different days and rates move, so compare again on the day you actually order.
  • To check mid-market: search “100 JPY to MYR”, or look at Wise or Bank Negara’s published rates.
  • Walking up to an airport counter, or changing money in Japan, is usually worse — pre-ordering online is normally the better of the three.
  • Don’t hold out for another 0.1% — missing the 4pm cut-off costs far more than the rate difference.

How much yen to order

The screenshot orders ¥120,000 ≈ RM3,155, about ¥15,000 a day over eight days. Since the tour already covers hotels and most main meals, that only makes sense if you plan to shop hard — and your shopping list is long, so it may well be right.

A practical split: keep the cash for what cards can’t do — rural shops, Suica top-ups, coin machines, the emergency taxi — and put the big shopping on a card. If you run short, 7-Eleven ATMs across Japan accept foreign cards.
If this is the group’s shared cash: log each person’s contribution in the guide’s tabung first, then record spending as you go — otherwise it’s unpickable afterwards.

After you collect

  • Count it at the counter and look at the mix of notes. ¥10,000 notes are awkward — rural shops often can’t break them.
  • Buying something small at a konbini breaks a big note painlessly — do it before you reach Ginzan.
  • Don’t keep it all in one place, and don’t put any of it in checked luggage.
  • Keep passport, boarding pass and cash separate — the passport comes out repeatedly for tax-free.
  • Leftover yen: spend it in airport duty-free or keep it for next time — changing it back loses another spread.
