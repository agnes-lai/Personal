Tabung — how the shared money pool works

Everyone puts money into one kitty, group costs come out of it, and the settle-up at the end works itself out. Every step folds away.

When to use Tabung, and when not to

SituationUse
You pool money up front and spend from it🏦 Tabung
Everyone pays separately and you square up later💸 Who owes who
Both happen (common)Use both, but log each expense in only one
Typical use: the yen cash you changed before the trip becomes the pool — taxis, shared snacks, coin lockers and tips come out of it, while personal shopping goes on your own card and never touches the tabung.

Step by step

1Open Trip Split, then the Tabung tabTwo different tools live here▾

Open 💰 Trip Split in the guide. There are two tabs at the top:

  • 💸 Who owes who — everyone pays for things themselves and you work out the debts afterwards. Good when one person covers a dinner and someone else covers a taxi.
  • 🏦 Tabung (pool) — everyone puts money into one shared kitty and group costs come out of it. That’s what this guide covers.
  • They are completely separate — entering something in one does not touch the other. Don’t log the same expense in both.
2Add everyone who has a share“Who is in the tabung” → type a name → + Add▾

Type each name under Who is in the tabung and press + Add. Once per person.

  • Use names everyone will recognise — they appear directly in the settle-up table at the end.
  • You can add people later. Spending already recorded is not recalculated to include them — past entries keep their original split.
  • Removing someone asks for confirmation. Their contributions stay in the log rather than vanishing.
3Money in — record each contributionPick who paid in, the amount, press Add contribution▾

Under ⬇ Money in: choose who paid in, enter the amount, add a remark if useful, then press Add contribution.

  • Record one entry per person. Don’t lump four people’s money into a single entry, or the final “gets back” column can’t be worked out.
  • The currency selector at the top applies to the next entry — set it before pressing the button.
  • Example: four people each put in ¥10,000 → four entries, pool total ¥40,000.
4Money out — and who actually shared itThe part that keeps it fair▾

Under ⬆ Money out: say what it was for, enter the amount, then look at the Who shared this? row of names.

  • It defaults to Everyone — for ordinary group costs just press the button, nothing to change.
  • When only some people shared it, tap off the ones who didn’t and leave only those who actually got the benefit.
  • The amount is split evenly between the people you leave selected.
  • You can’t deselect everybody — if you try, one stays selected, because a spend belonging to nobody would break the arithmetic.
  • The log then reads “shared by everyone” or “shared by A, B” — so an unusual refund can always be traced back to the entry that caused it.
5Read the settle-upPaid in · Used · Gets back▾

The The pool block shows the balance, then a table:

ColumnMeans
Paid inwhat this person put into the pool
Usedtheir share of the spending they were part of
Gets backpaid in − used. Negative means they still owe the pool
  • Below the table each person gets a line — “Give back to X” or “X still owes the tabung” — which you can just act on.
  • The last line checks itself: refunds must equal what’s left in the pool. If they ever don’t, it says so rather than quietly showing wrong numbers.
  • If spending exceeds the pool, the balance turns red and warns that someone has covered the gap out of pocket.
6Fixing mistakesDelete a single entry, or clear everything▾

Every row in the log has a × on the right. Pressing it removes that entry and the numbers recalculate immediately.

  • To change an amount: delete it and re-enter — there is no edit.
  • Clear the tabung wipes the whole pool on this device. It asks first, but don’t press it casually.
7Sharing it across phonesOne shared ledger, or one phone keeps it▾

The pool can live only on your phone, or be a shared ledger everyone can see.

  • There’s a status strip above the tabung with 🔄 Sync now and ✨ Start shared ledger from this device.
  • Editing the shared ledger needs unlocking (🔒 Enter password to edit). The password is deliberately not written in this guide — get it from whoever set the ledger up.
  • Simplest arrangement: one person records everything and the rest just look. Far fewer problems mid-trip.
  • It works offline — entries save locally and you press Sync once you have signal.

Why “who shared this” matters

Four people each put in ¥10,000 → pool ¥40,000. Four things are paid out, two of them only for some people:

SpendAmountShared by
Day 3 taxi¥6,000all 4
Group dinner¥9,000all 4
Snack run¥300Agnes, WK
Bee’s souvenir¥2,000Bee only

With “who shared this” filled in correctly, the result is:

PersonPaid inUsedGets back
Agnes¥10,000¥3,900¥6,100
WK¥10,000¥3,900¥6,100
Bee¥10,000¥5,750¥4,250
Sam¥10,000¥3,750¥6,250
Total¥40,000¥17,300¥22,700
If you skip it and split everything evenly: all four are charged ¥4,325 and all four get ¥5,675 back — Bee walks away ¥1,425 ahead, paid for by the other three.
The check: refunds add up to ¥22,700 and the pool holds ¥22,700 — balanced. That line is always shown, so the tabung can never quietly gain or lose money.

More than one currency

  • ' JPY, MYR and SGD are pooled separately — no exchange rate is applied and they are never added together.
  • Each currency gets its own settle-up table.
  • ' That’s deliberate — rates move daily and forcing a conversion is what starts arguments. If you really want one number, convert manually after the trip at an agreed rate.

Tips that save arguments

  • ' Record it on the spot. By the end of a day nobody remembers who the ¥300 snack was for.
  • ' Be specific: “Day 3 Ginzan taxi” is far easier to check later than “taxi”.
  • ' Log the contributions before you start spending, or the balance goes negative and looks alarming.
  • ' Keep personal shopping out of the pool. If it must go in, tick only that person — but at that point it’s simpler for them to pay themselves.
  • ' Do the settle-up on the last day while everyone is still together.
  • ' Unless you use the shared ledger, the pool lives only on the device that recorded it — don’t clear that browser’s data.
