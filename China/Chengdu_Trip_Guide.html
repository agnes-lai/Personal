Jiuzhaigou — the full day

关Airport & Customs Rules — Malaysia → Singapore → China → home

📱 0 · Phone & money — set up before you fly

  • Alipay is the main way to pay in China — link your Malaysian Visa/Mastercard and verify your passport in the app before leaving. Set up WeChat Pay too as a backup; a few small stalls only take WeChat.
  • Mobile data via Malaysian roaming or a travel eSIM usually routes outside China, so Google, WhatsApp and Instagram keep working. Hotel Wi-Fi does not — switch it off when you need those apps.
  • DiDi (inside Alipay, English interface) replaces Grab — Grab doesn’t operate in China. Amap 高德 is the most accurate map; Apple Maps in China uses the same data in English.
  • Keep ¥300–500 cash each for mountain toilets and village stalls.

1 · Leaving Malaysia (KLIA T1, Fri 15 Jan)

  • Counter by 19:00. Ask for bags tagged through to TFU.
  • Carry-on liquids: max 100 ml per bottle in one 1 L clear bag.
  • Power banks: hand-carry only, never checked. Singapore Airlines and Scoot don’t allow you to use or charge a power bank on board. Up to 100 Wh is fine; 100–160 Wh needs airline approval.

2 · Transit in Singapore (both ways)

  • You stay airside — no Singapore immigration, no baggage claim.
  • Carry-ons are screened again at the gate: duty-free liquids must stay in the sealed bag with the receipt inside.
  • Coming home, Changi duty-free is the easiest place to buy liquor — it goes straight onto the last flight.

3 · Entering China (TFU, Sat 16 Jan 06:55)

  • Visa-free for Malaysian passports — up to 30 days per entry under the China–Malaysia mutual visa exemption (in force since 17 Jul 2025). Passport should be valid 6+ months.
  • Arrival card: since 20 Nov 2025 you can fill it online before flying — NIA website, the ‘NIA 12367’ app, or the WeChat / Alipay mini-programme. Save the QR. You can also do it at the airport by QR or on paper. Address in China: 美沁酒店（成都宽窄巷子奎星楼店）.
  • Fingerprints + photo at the kiosks, then the officer. Nothing to declare → green channel.
  • Declare cash over ¥20,000 or foreign currency over US$5,000 equivalent. No fresh fruit, meat, eggs or plants.

4 · Inside China — passport every day

  • Every hotel registers every guest’s passport at check-in.
  • Scenic-area tickets are real-name: Bipenggou, Jiuzhaigou, the Panda Base, Wuhou Shrine. Carry the passport itself, not a photocopy.
  • Road checkpoints into the Aba mountain region can check passengers’ ID — passports in your day bag, not the van’s boot.

5 · Leaving China (TFU, Sat 23 Jan)

  • At the airport by 07:00 for the 10:00 flight.
  • No lighters or matches — banned on flights from China in both hand and checked luggage.
  • Power banks must show their capacity clearly (mAh / Wh). Unlabelled, swollen or damaged ones get confiscated at Chinese security.
  • Departure tax refund: shops with a ‘Tax Free / 离境退税’ sign issue a refund form (ask the minimum spend when you buy). Take the form + unused goods to the refund counter at TFU before emigration.

6 · Arriving home (KLIA T1)

  • Duty-free: 1 litre of alcohol per adult (non-Muslim, 18+) — more means declaring and paying duty.
  • Cigarettes: no duty-free allowance — declare any you carry.
  • Other new goods: roughly RM500 exemption per person — normal souvenir hauls are fine.
⚠ Meat warning: meat products need import permits and are commonly seized at Malaysian customs. That includes Sichuan favourites: 张飞牛肉 / 灯影牛肉 beef, 牦牛肉干 yak jerky, 腊肉 and sausages, and 牛油 beef-tallow hotpot base. Pick 清油 / 素 (vegetable-oil) hotpot base, tea, chilli bean paste, sweets and panda goods instead.
